One complete story · 2019–2021

Far Sector

A rookie Lantern. A city that has suppressed emotion. A murder that should not be possible.

Follow the one-book reading route

Start here

Commitment12 issues; one complete collected story.
ReadFar Sector #1–12, in numbered order.
CreatorsN.K. Jemisin (writer), Jamal Campbell (art), Deron Bennett (letters).

Prerequisites: none. You need only the basic idea that a Green Lantern uses a ring to turn imagination and will into protective force and constructs. This story introduces its own protagonist, society, and mystery. It belongs to the wider DC Universe, but it does not require Blackest Night or another crossover. Jo's later Corps adventures are a continuation of her career, not missing chapters of this book.

The official DC collection contains all twelve issues. The original series ran from 2019 to 2021; #12 is the finale. Read the collection's contents page when choosing an edition; cover format and publication date may differ.

A city worth understanding

Jo arrives in the City Enduring as an outsider asked to keep the peace. Its enormous population includes three peoples with different bodies and ways of living. Their apparent stability rests on removing emotion from everyday life. A killing therefore challenges both the police and the story the city tells about itself. Jo must work out who is harmed by that system and whose account deserves belief.

Her experience as a soldier and former New York police officer shapes the questions she asks. This is a book about the use of authority: when does enforcing an institution's rules protect people, and when does it protect the institution? Campbell's densely imagined city makes those questions physical, giving the inhabitants homes, public spaces, technology, and cultures rather than treating them as scenery. These are reading themes, not a substitute for discovering the suspects and their motives. DC's retrospective discusses Jo and the creative team.

Jo's ring has limits

Jo's experimental ring recharges itself rather than requiring the usual external power battery. That does not mean unlimited energy on demand. She has to manage what she spends, and this constraint matters when an investigation turns into a crisis. It also makes her a useful guide to Lantern lore: a ring is a tool with rules, not an automatic answer to every problem.

Jemisin confirms the lack of a battery and the accompanying limitations in DC's creator interview; DC also describes it as self-charging.

Spoilers: developments in the final two issues

The murder inquiry expands into a political crisis. In #11, unrest and a threatened attack on the population force Jo to act while her ring is running low. That combination brings the book's questions about policing, legitimacy, and material limits together. #12 completes the investigation; its solution is left for the reader to discover. DC's #11 description identifies this escalation.

After Far Sector

For Jo joining the wider ensemble, continue with Geoffrey Thorne's Green Lantern (2021). For another human-scale entry point, try Jessica Cruz and Simon Baz in Green Lanterns. Or visit Jo's profile for her place in the franchise. You can also stop here: this is a complete reading commitment.
