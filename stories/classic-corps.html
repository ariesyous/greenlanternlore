The classic Corps · 1980s

A universe beyond Hal

A three-issue adventure, three short stories, or an ensemble run: choose how far into the Corps you want to travel.

Back to the classics route

Start with the idea, not a continuity exam

The Guardians recruit ring-bearers from across the universe. Hal Jordan is one of them, and his perspective is only one way into the job. These selections explore what happens when a shared duty brings together people with different bodies, customs, and assumptions.

These are optional branches, not missing chapters of the Johns checklist. The 1981 miniseries and early backups precede Crisis on Infinite Earths; the later ensemble crosses into the post-Crisis period. Read their rules in their original context. They predate the modern spectrum framework, and publication dates do not fix dates inside the fiction.

One complete adventure: Tales of the Green Lantern Corps

Read: Tales of the Green Lantern Corps (1981) #1–3. Prerequisites: the basic ring-and-Guardians premise is enough; the story revisits the Corps' beginnings. Mike W. Barr supplies the plot, Len Wein the script, Joe Staton the pencils, and Frank McLaughlin the inks, with covers by Brian Bolland.

A summons to Oa brings the Lanterns together against a threat involving Krona. The attraction is the ensemble: the Corps has to work as a community, rather than merely provide backup for its human lead. Arisia enters the mythology here. DC's retrospective discusses the creative team and the story's place between the Silver Age and later Corps books.

Match your edition. The 2009 Tales of the Green Lantern Corps Vol. 1 collects the three issues plus backups from Green Lantern #148, #151–154, #161–162, and #164–167. The 2026 retrospective describes a different digital collection with annuals. The original three issues are the complete central adventure in either case.

Three short stories, three different questions

Try these in publication order. They are individual anthology stories, not three full-length event issues or a single continuing arc.

  1. “Mogo Doesn't Socialize” — Green Lantern (1960) #188 (1985), Alan Moore and Dave Gibbons. Begin here for a compact science-fiction puzzle about an elusive Corps member. DC identifies the issue and creators; its article reveals the answer, so read the story first if you want the discovery intact.
  2. “Tygers” — Tales of the Green Lantern Corps Annual #2 (1986), Alan Moore and Kevin O'Neill. Follow Abin Sur into the darker, prophetic side of the mythology. A useful reading question is how a prediction can shape the person who hears it. DC's reprint description identifies the original annual and creators.
  3. “In Blackest Night” — Tales of the Green Lantern Corps Annual #3 (1987), Alan Moore and Bill Willingham. Katma Tui must explain the Corps to sightless recruit Rot Lop Fan. It asks whether a shared institution needs everyone to experience its symbols identically. DC's oath history explains the sound-based interpretation; DC's creator interview identifies the Moore/Willingham story. This 1987 tale is distinct from the 2009 Blackest Night event.

One collection option: the 2006 DC Universe: The Stories of Alan Moore lists Green Lantern #188 and the two annuals. It is a broader DC anthology; check for those specific stories rather than expecting a book entirely about Lanterns.

Spoilers: connections to later lore and the short-story reveals

The 1981 miniseries introduces Nekron, whom Blackest Night later brings to the center of a larger event. Mogo's story reveals that the missing Lantern is the planet itself. Rot Lop Fan interprets the ring through sound and a bell rather than a visible lantern. These use the Corps premise for cosmic horror, a change in scale, and communication across alien experience. The original stories need not be read merely as setup for later uses of those ideas.

A longer ensemble: the 1980s Corps

If the alien cast is the appeal, try the Steve Englehart/Joe Staton period. Green Lantern Corps: Beware Their Power Vol. 1 (2018 edition) collects Green Lantern Corps #207–215 and Tales of the Green Lantern Corps Annual #2. Its main run follows team relationships alongside conflicts with the Rocket Red Brigade, Hector Hammond, and Star Sapphire.

Commitment: nine main-series issues plus the annual. This is a mid-run selection, not a new origin or the complete 1980s series. It overlaps the Moore route through Annual #2. Despite the publisher URL's “2025” series path, the listed 2018 edition and #207–215 contents identify the older material.

Our suggestion: read the three-issue Tales adventure, sample the short stories, then try the ensemble if you want more time with its cast. For a modern rebuilding story, continue with Recharge. For Hal's road trip on Earth, choose Hard-Traveling Heroes.
