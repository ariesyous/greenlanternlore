How to use the references

Issue references identify the series and publication year when titles repeat: Green Lantern (2005) #1 is a different book from Green Lantern (2021) #1. A cover date can differ from the day a comic went on sale; the reading guide orders stories and labels publication spans rather than treating those dates as events inside the fiction.

Sources beside the revised guides link to DC's issue listings, collected editions, and editorial retrospectives. Issue numbers support the reading sequence; publisher descriptions support the stated premise and collection contents. Commentary about a story's meaning is our interpretation. A bibliography is a starting point, not proof of every sentence in an older article.

New and revised guides separate a premise from optional plot outcomes, identify continuity, and mark their review date. Character histories are closed by default. Reading lists and character affiliations can still reveal that a character participates in a later story. For the distinctions between an original story, a later explanation, and a separate universe, see Which Green Lantern continuity?

Collection titles are reading references, not a promise of current stock or subscription availability. Compare the issue list in your edition; special issues and annuals are sometimes collected separately. The longer checklist is a selected route through the Lantern books, not every DC crossover appearance.

Editorial guidance reviewed November 4, 2026. Profile reference notes explain their scope: a debut reference, reading-list source, and fully supported biography are different things. Adding a reference does not certify every sentence of a legacy history. Suggest a correction with the page, series, issue, and relevant scene.

Art credits

The artwork is self-hosted, but the legacy credits are incomplete. A label such as “DC Comics via a wallpaper site” names neither the original comic nor the artist. Character creators and story writers are not automatically the creators of the portrait or cover shown beside them.

The image provenance manifest inventories 179 image files as of November 4, 2026. 9 files have a verified named cover-artist credit; 3 more are matched to a publisher image but still need cover-role confirmation; 167 remain unresolved. These are file counts, so two resolutions of the same cover count twice. Even a verified record can have an unknown colorist, local edition, or original download location; its remaining gaps are recorded explicitly.

Named cover credits established in this pass

  • Far Sector #1 (2019) — Jamal Campbell. DC's explicit cover credit. Newly downloaded from the publisher for the scanning guide.
  • Green Lanterns #20 (2017) — Robson Rocha and Daniel Henriques. DC's cover credits. Newly downloaded from DC's creator interview for the healing guide; cover colorist still unconfirmed.
  • Green Lanterns: Rebirth #1 (2016) — Ethan Van Sciver. DC's cover credit. The publisher reference matches the local artwork; trade dress differs.
  • Brightest Day #1 (2010) — David Finch. DC's main-cover credit. This does not claim a complete list of ink, color, or production contributors.
  • The Green Lantern #1 (2018) — Liam Sharp. DC's cover credit. The story and era pages use two resolutions of this artwork.
  • Green Lantern #1 (2011) — Ivan Reis and Joe Prado. DC's cover credits. Two local files reuse the Sinestro artwork; one is an Italian edition and the other has a different background color. Those edition and recoloring details remain unresolved.
  • Blackest Night #1 (2009) — cover pencils by Ivan Reis. DC's cover reveal specifically names his pencils; the cover inker and colorist remain unconfirmed in this manifest.

What still needs work

The covers for Green Lantern: Rebirth #1 and Green Lantern #29 and #63 have been visually matched to DC's images. Their records deliberately keep issue-level talent separate from cover-specific roles. The Sinestro Corps Special image on this site also differs from the version currently displayed by DC, so its exact variant or promotional origin is still unresolved.

All nine homepage Corps images, all eight entity illustrations, and the 70 character portraits (69 files in the character folder plus Alan Scott's era image) still need exact source and artist verification. Other outstanding files include panels, world illustrations, adaptation images, and the social preview. Generic existing captions are historical leads, not completed verification.

The two new power-guide illustrations are real published comic artwork, with their context explained in the captions. They are covers accompanying a discussion, not evidence that a pictured character is performing a particular scan or healing feat. No AI artwork was added in this pass.

The manifest records the file hash, pages using the image, publisher references where established, and remaining gaps. Credits identify provenance; they do not imply a license or publisher endorsement. For a correction or removal request, open an issue naming the image and, if known, its original publication and artist.
