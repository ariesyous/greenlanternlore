2009–2010

Blackest Night

The dead rise. The living have to work together.

Start here without the ending: The premise and reading plan below avoid the resolution. Plot history and later continuity changes are in a closed section.

Publication2009–2010
Main-series writerGeoff Johns
Main-series artIvan Reis, with Oclair Albert, Joe Prado and collaborators
CollectionBlackest Night contains #0–8; the Lantern tie-ins are separate collections.

This is a DC-wide event with Green Lantern mythology at its center. The main miniseries follows the shared crisis; Hal’s ongoing series and Green Lantern Corps tell different, concurrent fronts.

Why read it?

Black rings raise the dead and use familiar faces against the living. Hal Jordan and Barry Allen confront a crisis built around grief, while rival Lantern factions must decide whether they can cooperate. The horror depends on relationships as much as on the scale of the threat.

Watch for: the difference between an emotional connection and a weapon that imitates one. Knowing every deceased DC character is unnecessary, but brief familiarity with Hal, Barry and the spectrum helps. The event contains death, bodily horror and bereavement.

Before, during and after

  • Before — recommended: Sinestro Corps War, Rage of the Red Lanterns and Agent Orange establish the rival factions. The spectrum guide is a shorter primer.
  • Shortest complete route: Blackest Night #0, then #1–8.
  • Recommended Lantern route: add Green Lantern (2005) #43–52 using the interleaved issue guide.
  • Optional ensemble branch: Green Lantern Corps (2006) #39–47 follows the battle around Oa. Other event miniseries are character-focused expansions.
  • Next — Hal: Green Lantern #53–62. Optional DC-wide follow-up: Brightest Day #0–24. These are different books.

The matching volumes are Blackest Night: Green Lantern (#43–52) and Blackest Night: Green Lantern Corps (#39–47). Tales of the Corps is supplementary material, not a substitute for either volume.

Story history and aftermath — major spoilers

Life, death and the white light

Black Hand serves Nekron, whose campaign uses Black Lanterns to exploit the living’s emotions. The coalition learns that combining the spectrum can resist the black rings, but the final struggle reveals a larger source: the white light of life and its Entity. This revelation unfolds in Blackest Night #7–8.

Sinestro briefly joins with the Life Entity. Hal then helps turn the connection to life against Nekron, and the ending restores a selected group of the dead. It does not undo every death in DC history. The payoff belongs to the main Blackest Night miniseries; the Lantern tie-ins show important events occurring alongside it.

Brightest Day follows the returned characters and the white light’s purposes. Meanwhile, Hal’s own Green Lantern #53–62 follows the emotional entities toward War of the Green Lanterns. See the Brightest Day event contents and the separate Green Lantern: Brightest Day contents.
