A power ring turns thought into solid light. These are the most famous things anyone's ever thought of.

Hal Jordan

The Boxing Glove

The most famous construct in comics. Hal's go-to: a giant green fist that hits like a truck. As the man himself puts it — "just takes a little imagination."

Superman/Batman #29 (2006), interior page excerpt. Ethan Van Sciver (interior artist (publisher credit)); Guy Major (interior colorist (publisher listing; corroborated by issue index)). Artwork source & details.

Hal Jordan

The Gavel

When a fist won't do, Hal upgrades to a judge's gavel the size of a bus. Subtlety is for other Lanterns.

Illustration attribution under review. Artist and original source not fully established. Artwork source & details.

John Stewart

Built From the Inside Out

"John's constructs are built from the inside out. You look close, you can see every nut and bolt that makes it work. Nothing he creates is hollow." The architect's touch.

Green Lantern: Rebirth #6 (2005), cropped interior panel. Ethan Van Sciver (interior pencils); Moose Baumann (interior colors). Artwork source & details.

Sinestro & Arkillo

Blades of Fear

The Sinestro Corps doesn't build tools — it builds nightmares. Yellow energy blades, designed to terrify before they cut.

Sinestro #2 (2014), interior page. Dale Eaglesham (interior pencils); Jason Wright (interior colors). Artwork source & details.

Hal Jordan (Red Lantern)

Rage Plasma

Red Lanterns are known for corrosive rage-plasma. This scene shows red-ring Hal Jordan; it illustrates that state, rather than a shaped construct. Some Red Lanterns later learn to form constructs.

Hal Jordan as a Red Lantern in Green Lantern #37 (2009). Pencils: Ivan Reis. This transformation illustration provides context for rage-powered abilities; it is not a demonstration of a specific construct. Artwork source & details.

How constructs work

Every construct is limited by two things: the wielder's imagination and their emotional control. Hal Jordan thinks in blunt instruments. John Stewart thinks in blueprints. Kyle Rayner thinks in illustrations. Sinestro thinks in your worst nightmares. The ring doesn't choose the weapon — the mind does.
