Compassion

Indigo Tribe

The strangest light of all — compassion, forced upon the compassionless. Its saints are reformed monsters.

Artwork: DC Comics via CBR — non-commercial fan tribute.

The Oath (in the tribe's own tongue)

Tor lorek san, bor nakka mur, natromo faan tornek wot ur. Ter Lantern ker lo Abin Sur, taan lek lek nok — Formorrow Sur!

No translation exists — only their word "Nok," meaning "compassion be with you."

The Emotion

Compassion — the indigo light doesn't find the kind-hearted. It finds the worst killers and sadists in the universe and forces them to feel remorse for everything they've done.

It's compassion as rehabilitation at ring-point: wrenching, involuntary, and weirdly effective.

Base of Operations

Nok — a harsh world where the tribe was born, far from the Guardians' oversight.

It was on Nok that the Green Lantern Abin Sur, alongside the rebel leader Natromo, first harnessed the indigo light — and where the first recruit was remade.

Notable Members

Indigo-1— born Iroque; once an assassin, now the tribe's first and most devoted lantern. Munk— the tribe's seer-like second, rarely seen without his staff. N Natromo— co-founder alongside Abin Sur; keeper of the tribe's origins.

The Staff & Ring

The tribe wields power staffs topped with lanterns rather than relying on rings alone. An indigo ring compels its bearer to feel only compassion — remove it from the unwilling, and the monster returns.

Quirks: indigo light can channel the powers of other Corps, force the remorseless to relive their victims' pain, and its bearers teleport across space in pillars of violet-indigo light.

Lore

Abin Sur — the great Green Lantern who came before Hal Jordan — discovered the indigo light while aiding a rebellion on Nok. He and Natromo forged the first indigo ring and gave it to Iroque, an assassin who had murdered Abin Sur's own daughter. The ring broke her, remade her, and she became Indigo-1.

Ever since, the tribe has "recruited" the galaxy's worst — even Sinestro and Black Hand have worn the indigo. It's the spectrum's hardest question asked as a Corps: is compassion still compassion if it's forced?
