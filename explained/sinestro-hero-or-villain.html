Sinestro: Hero or Villain?

The greatest Green Lantern became the Corps’ greatest enemy. Or did he just stop pretending the Guardians were right?

Ask a Green Lantern fan to name the greatest villain in the mythos and most will say Sinestro before you finish the question. Ask them to name the greatest Green Lantern and a surprising number will say the same name. Both answers are correct — and that contradiction is the whole point of the character.

The model Lantern

When Thaal Sinestro debuted in Green Lantern (vol. 2) #7 (1961), John Broome and Gil Kane didn't introduce a cackling evildoer. They introduced the Corps' pride: the Lantern of Sector 1417, a mentor so respected that the Guardians assigned him to train the newly chosen Hal Jordan. Sinestro was what every Green Lantern was supposed to be — disciplined, fearless, utterly devoted to order.

That devotion was the problem. On his homeworld of Korugar, Sinestro decided that order required control — total control. He deposed the planet's rulers and installed himself as dictator, ruling through fear while genuinely believing he was saving his people from themselves. When a Korugarian called him power-mad, Sinestro killed him for it. The Guardians stripped his ring and exiled him to the antimatter universe of Qward (the full story).

The prophet of fear

Exile didn't humble Sinestro; it clarified him. On Qward, the Weaponers forged him a yellow ring — powered by fear, the one color the green light couldn't touch — and Sinestro built the Sinestro Corps on a single thesis: the Guardians' way had failed, and only terror could keep the universe in line. The Sinestro Corps War (2007) was his proof of concept, and it nearly worked.

Here's what makes him impossible to dismiss as a simple villain: he has a point. The Guardians' ten billion years of "order" produced the Manhunters, the Massacre of Sector 666, and a Corps riddled with their own failures. Sinestro looked at that record and concluded that willpower without fear is naivety. He's wrong — but he's wrong in a way that forces the heroes to be better.

The uncomfortable question

Modern Sinestro is even harder to categorize. During the Wrath of the First Lantern (2013), it was Sinestro — wielding both a green and a yellow ring — who killed Volthoom and ended the threat, at the cost of executing the Guardians themselves afterward. He has saved the universe and doomed worlds, mentored heroes and murdered friends, sometimes in the same storyline.

So: hero or villain? The honest answer is that Sinestro is the Green Lantern mythos asking its central question out loud — what is order worth, and who gets to decide? The Guardians answered with control. Hal Jordan answered with will. Sinestro answered with fear. The fact that his answer keeps almost working is why, sixty years on, he's still the most interesting person in the room.
