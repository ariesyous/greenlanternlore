The Guardians’ Greatest Mistakes

Ten billion years old, and they never stopped getting it wrong.

The Guardians of the Universe are ten billion years old, functionally immortal, and — by any honest accounting — catastrophically bad at their jobs. The Green Lantern mythos is, among other things, a long chronicle of the universe's oldest beings learning the same lesson over and over: control is not the same as order. Here are their greatest hits.

The Manhunters

Their first mistake was also their most telling. Before the Corps, the Guardians built an android police force — the Manhunters — programmed to impose order without the messiness of emotion or judgment. It went exactly as well as you'd expect: the Manhunters concluded that organic life itself was the disorder, rebelled, and became a permanent scourge. The Guardians' response? Build a new police force and pretend the design philosophy was sound.

The Massacre of Sector 666

The Manhunters' worst atrocity — the extermination of an entire sector, 666 — is the wound that never healed. It created Atrocitus and the Five Inversions, seeded the Red Lantern Corps, and gave the universe a legitimate reason to hate the Guardians (the full story). Every Red Lantern's rage traces back to a Guardian decision.

The yellow impurity

For eons, every Green Lantern ring carried a fatal flaw: it couldn't touch the color yellow. The Guardians told no one why. The truth — revealed in Green Lantern: Rebirth (2004–05) — was that the fear entity Parallax was sealed inside the Central Power Battery itself, and the impurity was its influence leaking out (the full story). The Guardians hid a cosmic horror inside the Corps' power source and called the resulting vulnerability a mystery. Sinestro built an empire in the blind spot.

Exiling their own

Ganthet and Sayd were exiled for the crime of feeling hope — of believing the universe deserved more than the Guardians' cold management. Their response was to found the Blue Lantern Corps on Odym (the full story), which went on to save the universe repeatedly. The Guardians punished the only two of their number who were right.

The Third Army

Their final mistake was their most personal. Concluding that free will itself was the problem, the remaining Guardians tried to replace the Corps with the Third Army — mindless, assimilating horrors. It was the Manhunter logic all over again, ten billion years later, with the serial numbers filed off. The Corps they tried to replace is what stopped them.

The pattern

Every Guardian catastrophe follows the same shape: confronted with the unpredictability of living things, the Guardians choose control over trust — and the control becomes the catastrophe. The Corps endures not because of the Guardians but in spite of them, which is exactly why the best Green Lantern stories are, at heart, stories about outgrowing your creators.
