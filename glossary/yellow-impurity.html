Yellow Impurity

The flaw that was a lie that was a prison.

The story

For most of the Green Lantern Corps' history, every Lantern carried the same inexplicable weakness: the ring could not directly affect anything yellow. A yellow wall stopped a green battering ram; a yellow bullet could kill a Lantern through his shields. The Corps called it the yellow impurity — a flaw in the green light itself, accepted as the price of power. Sinestro, who understood the flaw better than any living being, built his entire war against the Corps around it, arming himself and then his whole Corps in the one color the green light could not touch.

The truth was worse than a flaw. Sealed in the heart of the Central Power Battery on Oa was Parallax — the living embodiment of fear, imprisoned by the Guardians at the dawn of the Corps. Its yellow essence bled into the green light, tainting every ring ever forged. The Guardians knew, and said nothing; the impurity was the seal on their prison, and the Corps paid its price in blood for three billion years. Hal Jordan's fall in Emerald Twilight was Parallax's influence flowering at last.

The impurity was purged in Green Lantern: Rebirth (2004–05, Johns/Van Sciver), when the truth was dragged into the light and Parallax was exorcised from the Battery — and from Hal Jordan. Modern rings bear no yellow weakness; a Green Lantern today fears no color. But the impurity's long shadow remains: it is the reason the Sinestro Corps exists, the reason the Corps learned to doubt its masters, and the proof of the Guardians' oldest sin — that they would cripple their own champions rather than admit what they had buried. The fear is gone from the light. The memory is not.
